Trevan hadn't done much investigating, apparently. It wasn't comforting, either, when he suggested I hand my hard-earned materials for the Wolfsbane over to a practically anonymous blacksmith in Tarren Mill.

It was a long walk through the entire length of Silverpine Forest and the better part of Hillsbrad just to reach the town—a Horde enclave carved into contested territory.

Ott was his name. I introduced myself and told him Trevan had sent me for a custom job. He was impressed by the collection of materials, and by the careful way he handled each piece, it was obvious he knew their worth.

While he inspected them, I noticed a rack of weapons in the back of his humble hut. They looked remarkably fine. The blacksmith in me couldn't help testing a few—feeling the balance in my grip, the counter-weight of the pommel, and the clean springiness of the steel.

After thoroughly examining my materials, Ott laid out his exact plan for the forge. He seemed genuinely honored by the chance to work with such noble components.

He told me to come back later. After spending a few hours running errands and helping the locals around Tarren Mill, I returned to his hut.

There it was: the finest blade I had ever seen.

Knowing how veteran paladins care for their weapons, Ott urged me not to wield it just yet—not until I consulted Trevan on whatever special treatment the sword still needed to undergo.

Upon my return, Trevan examined the weapon closely. It was a "virgin" blade, and I was wise to keep it that way because the enchantment he was thinking of needed the blood of a specially powerful beast. He exchanged looks with Lumina at his side, and both said, "Old Fire-Eye."

That almost anonymous bladesmith was an expert on swords. He knew how to work with those rare materials and even knew to take into consideration the needs of a paladin. I should have trusted the experience and expertise of both Ott and Trevan.

And I should know better than to pass judgment on things I know nothing about.
