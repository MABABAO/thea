If the Undercity is a marvel of engineering and organization, Orgrimmar is something else altogether.

The scale of the Orcish capital is staggering. The city is colossal, built of massive open plazas and towering structures of wood, iron, and bone. The Orcs themselves are massive, and their architecture reflects it. Unlike the cramped, singular merchants of the Eastern Kingdoms, they don’t just have scattered auctioneers; they have a dedicated, roaring Auction House. The bank itself feels like a fortress.

Navigating the immense distances is exhausting, but thankfully, the citizens in the streets are remarkably willing to point a lost traveler in the right direction. I immediately began mingling in the valley hubs, seeking out those in need of aid to earn my keep. Did I mention the sheer scale of the place? The Orcs have lakes contained within their city walls!

And so happened I hooked up with some guys that had similar business with Varimathras, the demon that asked me to retrieve some books for the Banshee Queen. I cannot help but wonder just how many identical errands he has handed out - and exactly how little he trusted me to get the job done. Maybe because those books were in the possession of hellish creatures lurking inside Orgrimmar's private hell?

Ragefire Chasm.

The name fits perfectly. It is a terrifying chasm carved directly into the volcanic rock beneath the city, filled with gaping fire pits, rushing rivers of molten lava, and incredibly dangerous creatures.

I began to wonder why that awful place was even allowed to exist. Could it be that their brutal warrior culture preserved that hellhole as a proving ground for would-be warriors to test their mettle? Not sure, though, an untamed piece of hell right below the city doesn't seem wise. Unless a constant flux of eager young champions diving down there keeps the danger from overflowing into the streets above.

I ended up joining a group of four of these champions. Those Horde types really love their grand titles.

We organized ourselves using a tactical loop I hadn't fully encountered before. One of us would actively draw the aggression of the chasm's creatures, while another focused entirely on weaving spells to heal him. We -the other three- had the straightforward task of dealing as much raw damage as possible in order to save our designated "tank" from further injuries.

The beasts down there are not idiots, though. They quickly sense the deception, and sometimes they switch targets to go straight for our healer or a soft, cloth-wearing ally.

My instinct was to rush to their defense. If a tactical stun didn't work, I would use a Blessing of Protection. When the creatures realized they couldn't get through my defenses, they would turn on me.

But then I heard the tank calling out, telling me I was making the healer's life harder. It took me a few moments to understand why: I was forcing him to heal both me and the tank.

Well, if the tank was already taking the heat, who was I to take away his fun?

Anyways, I still don't know what our tank did or said to those monsters, but whatever it was, he really got them infuriated.

Deep inside, we encountered several bosses, for lack of a better word. They seemed to be the sinister masterminds behind the entire subterranean operation, and they were mostly warlocks.

I will tell you this much: if the Dark Lady back home doesn't trust us paladins, those Orcs have a massive, deep-seated bone to pick with warlocks. I will have to poke around the city later to truly understand the history behind this hatred.

As it happens, though, there are apparently good warlocks and bad warlocks. Down in a dark corner inside the Cleft of Shadow, I crossed paths with one named Neeru Fireblade. He asked me to bring him the severed head of the Searing Blade leader - a massive, terrifying Felguard named Taragaman the Hungerer - claiming it was necessary to appease the Horde's Warchief, Thrall.

So I did.
