I am back in Brill, still trying to make sense of Undercity.

My, oh my, the Undercity.
It is an overwhelming, labyrinthine metropolis buried deep beneath the ruins of Lordaeron. I spent hours poring over books in the dusty corners of the city, and the local innkeeper filled in the horrific gaps of my missing history. Arthas and his wretched Scourge left nothing standing above ground.

I tried to act like a seasoned traveler. I opened a bank account to keep my few precious belongings safe, bartered with the locals, and put some wild herbs I gathered along the road up for auction. But gathering weeds won't be my future; it is just a means to an end. While walking through the smog-choked quarters, I watched the heavy hammers of the blacksmiths striking glowing metal. I realized then that I could learn to forge my own weapons. True plate armor, not these mismatched scraps. But ore is expensive. If I want the metal, I will have to mine it from the earth myself.

I also crossed paths with a fellow holy soul - a priestess. She is in a far worse state than I am, possessing absolutely no memory of her past life, though the Light still answers her call. Barely. She has given herself a new name. I won't write it in these pages in case someone steals this diary; it is shockingly, hilariously obscene. But beneath the crude title, she was a sweetheart. We struck a deal: if I send her magical equipment I find on my travels, she can disenchant it and sell the magical essences at the auction house so we both can make a profit. She told me that this would be a long-term arrangement. She doesn't have the capital to acquire magic items and disenchant them, sending her such items would help her develop that skill. The higher that skill, she explained, the likelier to obtain more and better magical essences from the items, so she has all the incentives to pay me handsomely and keep me sending her more. I liked her honesty.

Moving through the capital wasn't easy. I quickly learned to wrap myself in tattered rags. Everyone in Undercity seems to love them, and it helped me blend in. Without the shroud, people stared at my golden eyes. Some looked absolutely terrified; others looked at me with venomous contempt. To survive the crowds, I forced myself to extinguish my Aura and spoke in a harsh, guttural voice. The people back in Brill seem much more accepting of a holy warrior. In the capital, I learned to be careful.

Finding an experienced paladin in that subterranean maze felt impossible until I swallowed my pride. I watched citizens asking a massive, stitched-together abomination for directions. He seemed remarkably pleasant, so I took my chances. The giant creature was incredibly helpful - he even drew a map pointing me directly to the paladin trainers. I had met a kind abomination near Brill before, but the ones in the city truly go out of their way for travelers.

On the long walk back to Brill, I couldn't stop thinking about what I had seen. A city bursting with life.
It sounds entirely contradictory, but the Forsaken are far more than a legion of corpses. I didn't see a single mindless ghoul wandering the upper quarters of the city. Instead, I saw a diverse, thriving society - people scraping together a living, trading goods, mastering complex professions, and simply moving forward with their days. Perhaps I missed the beauty of it when I first woke up in Deathknell. Everything was too terrifying and confusing then.
Now, I see clearly. There are rogues - who aren't nearly as menacing as I feared (perhaps the truly dangerous ones simply stay hidden). There are shamans, hunters, and priests. The warlocks still give me the absolute creeps, I won't lie, but they have their place. Even the living - trolls, tauren, and orcs - walk the streets alongside us. They actually have pulses.
Sylvanas truly did something miraculous here. She took the shattered, haunted ruins of a fallen kingdom, tore us free from the Lich King’s horrific mental grasp, and forged a home for them. She gave US a home.
