Life was easy, and I never noticed it.

In hindsight, the ease of Tirisfal Glades should have been obvious. The Undercity stands as both a monumental military achievement and a marvel of engineering, and its shadow has effectively tamed the surrounding valleys. Anyone gathering copper, plucking peacebloom, or quietly honing their leatherworking and enchanting skills soon learns the predictable rhythms of the local wildlife. If a threat grows too large for the populace to handle, the military might of the Horde simply steps in and wipes it out.

Silverpine Forest, however, is an entirely different beast.

For one, the Kirin Tor and their looming fortress of Dalaran sit right across the road. 

Wild boars and plaguebats are predictable; they do not shoot crackling fireballs from the tips of wands. Some of the coast-dwelling murlocs do, of course, but they are merely cute - dangerously, fiendishly cute. 

The wizards of Dalaran, on the other hand, possess a terrifyingly real power directed at the Horde.

Smack in the middle of this tense woodland sits the Sepulcher. It operates like a mini-Brill, an enclave with its own desperate needs. Yet these needs are not dictated by a local civilian populace, but by the cold, strategic demands of the Horde war machine - demands that constantly clash with the Alliance, Dalaran, and the Kirin Tor.

It is brutal, uncompromising warcraft out there. I could barely complete a handful of missions before hitting a wall of sheer impracticality. Elite troops patrol the main roads, and something far more sinister stirs in the deep woods. Beastly figures that look like giant wolves walk upright on two legs, wielding fangs and claws that tear through plate armor far harder than any Alliance footman I met.

So I have learned that all I have been doing is called tank-and-spank. Until Silverpine, fighting had been a rather orderly business. I knew which blessings to call upon, when to judge an enemy, when to stun one, and when to heal myself. I had developed a comfortable rhythm, and most enemies were obliging enough to fall over before I had to change it.

Silverpine shattered that complacency. Now, even a simple task like chipping away at a tin ore node requires keeping my head on a swivel for wandering patrols or sudden ambushes. Fighting means watching an enemy’s hands closely to interrupt an incoming healing spell, choosing the exact right judgment for the moment, and preventing them from running off to pull reinforcements.

When I partner with other adventurers, the mental clutter multiplies. I am suddenly responsible for keeping track of everyone else - cleansing a curse, throwing an emergency flash of Light, or shifting my blessings to match a companion's fighting style. Just the other day, our line collapsed. I had to instantly swap my two-handed weapon for a shield and mace, emergency heal a wounded warrior, bless a panicking priest, and hold my ground while trying to bring down a ... werewolf, I guess?

Now, the local command expects me to storm two fortified strongholds - one overrun by Gnolls, and another packed with Kirin Tor magi. No can do. 

Not because I am a lazy gal. I tried to storm that gnoll fortress twice, in fact. Both times, I joined other adventurers who had unfinished business there. Apparently, that stronghold has been a thorn in many people's sides. Both times, we had to turn tail and flee. The gnolls were far too organized and well-equipped, as the scattered remains of previous attackers could attest.

Where else can I further hone my skills? In another tamed land! 

Orgrimmar.
