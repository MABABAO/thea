I met this paladin on my second trip to Orgrimmar.

He seemed like a remarkably experienced adventurer, and these days, I have learned to read and measure an adventurer simply by looking at his gear. Making my own armor gave me that understanding; workmanship tells me about quality; wear and repairs suggest what they have survived.

I asked him about training grounds and my idea to roam Durotar as a way to level up my skills. He smiled and told me I was on the right track. He noted that my armor looked good enough, but he thought I might use a more suitable blade. I thought my Fallen blade was just fine, but I told him to continue. I am into blades now, apparently.

He told me his own blade is called Wolfsbane, and that it was blessed by the Light.

Of course, that sparked my full attention.

I had a real hard time killing that thing in Silverpine - the one that looked like an upright-walking wolf. I had to use all the wits I hardly knew I had just to stay alive and save my partners in that claustrophobic mine. I told him about my encounter, and his smile faded slightly.

He told me I had met a Worgen. Very few meet one and get to tell the tale.

He explained how I could acquire a blade such as his. In fact, he advised me to seek it out. But he warned that I should be prepared to be tested in many different ways. As it happens, there's always a "but".

"Someone told me not so long ago that the Light tests the vessel," he told me. "Remember that."

"There's no amount or quality of information that could prepare you for the true horrors of Silverpine Forest," he continued. "I too thought the mages of Dalaran or that Gnoll nuisance were the worst Silverpine had to offer. Tell me, who do you think can better guide you for that?"

The answer came without thinking, as if I already knew what I was to be asked.

"Commander Danitha Morr."

"You have been paying attention. Good." And he smiled again.

We talked some more about politics, weather, and the next adventures he was seeking in Kalimdor.

As we arrived at the outskirts of Orgrimmar, he bid me farewell - and to my absolute horror, he casually jumped right off the side of the moving Zeppelin! I ran to the railing, fully expecting to watch his corpse get plastered across the red clay below, when I noticed he had landed perfectly on the platform of the lower Zeppelin tower. From there, another quick jump, and he was off.

Experience has its value.
